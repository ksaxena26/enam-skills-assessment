Executive Summary

Investment Style & Profile

Over a 9.67-year horizon, this four-stock portfolio delivered a cumulative gross return of +1,246% on closed and marked-to-market capital, compounding at 30.8% p.a. (time-weighted) against the NiftyBeES benchmark's 11.6%. On a money-weighted basis — reflecting actual capital deployment timing — the return was approximately 26% p.a.

The investor is best described as a patient trend-following position-builder: entries cluster in the lower third of each stock's price band, follow-on buys predominantly pyramid into strength rather than against it, and winning positions are held for multi-year periods. Churn is low. The style is low-activity, high-conviction, and structurally oriented toward letting gains compound.

"The portfolio earned roughly 2.7× the index's annual return at 2.6× its volatility — yielding a Sharpe of 0.72 against the benchmark's 0.40, with a still-wider Sortino advantage."

That risk-adjusted edge, however, comes with a specific character: the returns are episodic, not steady. Four years (2017, 2020, 2023, 2024) carry almost the entire outperformance; in between sit a −27% year (2018), a −45% year (2022), and a near-flat 2025. The cost of this style is a −59.5% maximum drawdown — extreme by any conventional mandate standard.

Performance

Cumulative & Annualized Returns

The portfolio compounds dramatically steeper than the benchmark across the full period, though with pronounced episodic drawdowns — most sharply in 2018 (Dilip Buildcon de-rated from a 2017 peak) and in 2022 (a simultaneous selloff across Zee, Amber, and DBL that produced a −44.9% calendar year while the index was flat).

The running annualized return (CAGR) demonstrates how the portfolio's advantage compounds over time. After volatile early years driven by Dilip Buildcon, the CAGR stabilizes well above 25% from 2020 onward as Amber's multi-year compounding becomes the dominant factor.

Risk-Adjusted Performance

Sharpe Ratio Analysis

The full-period Sharpe of 0.72 compares favourably to the benchmark's 0.40. The Sortino ratio gap is wider still — 1.13 vs 0.50 — confirming that the portfolio's elevated headline volatility (37.9%) is disproportionately upside volatility. The investor absorbs more risk than the index, but that risk is asymmetrically rewarded.

The rolling Sharpe captures the portfolio's character plainly: a strong 2017 (Dilip Buildcon ×4), a deep negative patch through 2018–19 as DBL de-rated, recovery and outperformance through 2020–21 as Amber re-rated, a sharp 2022 trough at rolling Sharpe ≈ −2 when concentration risk fully materialised, and a sustained period above 1.0 through 2023–24 as Amber reached its peak compounding phase.

Action Items

Recommendations

  1. 01
    Fix the sizing engine — the largest and most tractable improvement available.

    In 3 of 4 names, larger lots underperformed smaller ones — the conviction signal driving "bet bigger" is anti-predictive on this evidence. Pre-commit position size at entry using a rules-based framework (volatility-adjusted fractions or tiered conviction bands) and measure size-vs-outcome correlation annually. The target is to move that correlation from negative to zero, then positive.

  2. 02
    Apply the same loser-cut discipline used on Amber to every position, in writing.

    Amber's losers were cut at a median 91 days — model behaviour. Zee's losers were ridden to 356 days with 26 consecutive average-downs — the opposite. A mechanical rule ("no adding to a position down more than X% from cost; thesis-invalidation stop within Y months") would have contained the Zee experience without requiring a discretionary call.

  3. 03
    Improve exit capture on winners with a trailing-stop or predefined scale-out ladder.

    Amber exits averaged the 41st percentile of the stock's full price range — significant upside was left unrealized. A rules-based trailing stop on the core position would capture more of the move without requiring a discretionary call at a market peak — the hardest judgment to make correctly.

  4. 04
    Formalise single-name concentration as a deliberate risk budget, not an emergent outcome.

    One name producing 92% of total profit is both a triumph and a structural fragility — 2022's −45% year (while the index was flat) quantifies the tail cost. Decide explicitly whether this is a "best-idea concentrated" strategy (entirely valid) or a diversified multi-stock one, then enforce the corresponding position limits.

  5. 05
    Codify the hold-discipline that created the returns — before the next winner arrives.

    Riding Amber and Welspun for years through −50%+ portfolio drawdowns is the rarest skill in this ledger. Write down in advance what would invalidate the investment thesis (not a price level — a business condition), and commit to holding until that condition is met. This prevents the disposition effect, which already appears on Zee, from migrating to future winning positions.

Methodology
Daily time-weighted returns: holdings marked to daily close; external buy/sell cash flows neutralized (daily TWR / Modified Dietz approximation). Pre-listing Amber Enterprises shares (purchased Dec-2017, listed Jan-2018) are valued at cost (₹800.6/sh) until market prices are available, correctly attributing the listing-day price discovery to the listing date rather than the purchase date. Sharpe and Sortino ratios are annualized from daily returns using 252 trading days; rf = 6% p.a. Full-period metrics cover 29-Jul-2016 → 31-Mar-2026 (9.67 years). Rolling Sharpe uses a 252-day trailing window. All figures are gross of taxes, STT, and brokerage costs — source trade amounts equal Qty × Rate. Benchmark = NiftyBeES (Nifty-50 ETF, used as a listed Indian large-cap proxy). Unrealized P&L on open positions is marked at 31-Mar-2026 closing prices: Amber ₹6,551 · DBL ₹388 · Zee ₹72 · Welspun ₹109. FIFO matching from the trade ledger is used to classify each lot as realized (matched buy-sell pair) or open (unmatched buy).
